Datum Interferogram

Two published coordinate operations, differenced across a continent. Colour cycles once per fixed increment of ground displacement, so you read the magnitude by counting fringes — the way an InSAR interferogram is read. Where the fringes crowd together, the two models are diverging quickly across space.

Fringe density is the gradient, not the size. A pure translation — two models that disagree by the same amount everywhere — produces a flat field with almost no fringes, however large that disagreement is. Structure appears only where the disagreement changes across space, which is where rotation, scale and regional operations live.
Cyclic data wants a cyclic colour map. A rainbow is the wrong choice for magnitude and the right one for phase, because the quantity itself wraps. Switch to magnitude and the map becomes a single-hue ramp, which is what that quantity needs.
This compares operations, not truth. A featureless field means two models agree, not that either is correct. Deciding that needs a control point or an independent reference, and no amount of metadata substitutes for one.
Real measurements. Every value was computed by transforming the same geographic grid through two published operations on a live QGIS 4.2.2 / PROJ 9.8 install and measuring the ellipsoidal distance between the results. 132 × 96 samples per region. Nothing here is simulated.
Ballpark fields behave differently by region. Over Australia the ballpark offset is enormous but nearly uniform — a near-pure translation, so it is almost fringe-free. Over Europe it ranges from 42 m to 189 m and fills with fringes. Same category of failure, completely different signature, and only the second one looks alarming.
